# Product Specification

## Product statement

Spanish OS is a public, practical Spanish-learning website for Traditional Chinese beginners. Learners unlock real-life situations and become able to complete useful tasks in Spanish from Spain through five-minute sessions and adult-friendly gamification.

## Language and audience

- Learner: Traditional Chinese speaker at CEFR pre-A1 or A1.
- Interface/explanations: Traditional Chinese.
- Target language/audio: Spain Spanish (`es-ES`).
- Prefer Spain vocabulary and `vosotros/as`. Show Latin American differences only when useful.

## Core learning loop

1. State one concrete can-do goal.
2. Introduce a small amount of useful language.
3. Practice recognition and recall through varied interactions.
4. Complete a realistic communication task.
5. Award progress and schedule weak items for review.

One scenario takes about 20 minutes and contains four approximately five-minute lessons:

1. Key language: normally 6–8 new words.
2. Comprehension: short `es-ES` audio and likely questions.
3. Expression: 1–2 core patterns and one small grammar idea.
4. Real-life task: an integrated staged or branching interaction.

## Initial scenario roadmap

1. **Meeting someone:** greet, state name/origin/languages, and ask the other person.
2. **Café and restaurant:** read essentials, order, express needs, and pay.
3. **Transport and directions:** buy a ticket, confirm service, ask where something is, and understand basic directions.
4. Shopping and markets.
5. Accommodation.
6. Social plans and invitations.
7. Health and emergencies.

The first scenario ends with a language-exchange conversation in Madrid. Its lessons cover greetings; names and `Encantado/a`; origin, residence, and languages; then the complete conversation.

## Exercises and answers

MVP types: multiple choice, matching, sentence ordering, short text input, and constrained dialogue. Later: listening-only work and constrained role-play. Speech recognition and pronunciation scoring are not MVP.

Ignore harmless capitalization/punctuation variation. Accept understandable regional forms with a note such as “可以理解；在西班牙更常說…”. Feedback explains the communicative distinction, not merely “wrong.”

## Progress and gamification

- Scenario states: learning, can cope, proficient.
- Rewards: XP, short combos, unlock animations, capability stamps, daily practical tasks, and low-pressure streaks.
- Mastery requires delayed successful recall, not one perfect attempt or accumulated XP.
- Approximately 70% may unlock progress; weak items enter review.
- No punitive lives, streak shame, or pay-to-learn advantages.

## MVP surfaces

Today page, practical scenario route, scenario detail, four micro-lessons, exercise player with normal/slow audio, real-life conversation, results/review suggestions, ability page, review queue, and guest first lesson with later registration for sync.

Explicitly outside MVP: leaderboards, friends/guilds, stores, unrestricted AI chat, pronunciation scoring, and full authoring CMS.

Measure learning through scenario completion, delayed recall, falling hint usage, successful real-life tasks, and return after missed days—not vanity engagement alone.
